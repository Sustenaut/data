# Using data.sustenaut.eu

[data.sustenaut.eu](https://data.sustenaut.eu) is the team's place to explore
public data about renewable energy markets. Its first page shows the French
auctions of guarantees of origin.

## Signing in

The site is for the Sustenaut team. You sign in with your team account at
auth.sustenaut.eu. If the site says you have no access, ask an administrator
to give you the **data** role.

## The GO auctions page

Every month, EEX auctions the guarantees of origin of French renewable power:
certificates that say a megawatt-hour came from wind, solar, hydro or thermal
plants in a given region. EEX publishes one average price per auction, region
and technology. One such price is a **segment**, and every chart on the page
is drawn from segments. A segment that sold nothing has no price, so the
charts leave it out, and the table under each chart counts how many.

The page has three tabs:

- **Price distribution**: how segment prices spread, per technology. You can
  count each segment once or weigh it by the megawatt-hours it sold, show
  shares or counts, overlay the technologies or give each its own panel, and
  choose the number and spacing of the bins.
- **Prices over time**: one point per auction and technology, with the
  lowest, typical and highest regional prices. Hover over a point to see its
  numbers.
- **By region**: a map of France's regions, coloured by the price or volume
  you choose.

The **Filters** panel on the left applies to all three tabs: the dates (by
auction date or by production month), the technologies (or all of them
combined), the regions, and, under *Auction conditions*, the auction numbers,
how much of the offer sold, the number of bidders, and the size of the
segments. Collapse the panel with the « arrows at its top.

## Light and dark

Open the ⋮ menu at the top right, then **Settings**, to choose a light or a
dark look, or to follow your computer's setting.

## How fresh the data is

The data is refreshed every Thursday morning, after EEX's auction day. The
caption under the page title says when the data was built, and links to the
run that built it.

## Something looks wrong

Tell the team, saying which page, which filters you had on, and what you
expected to see. A mistake in EEX's own files is recorded and corrected in
the data, and can be reported back to EEX.
